Field note / 01

You don’t need to know what the problem is called.

Every organization runs on plumbing. We work on the kind that carries information — from the person who has it to the person who needs it, from the tool where it’s entered to the place where it’s used. When those pipes are clear, work flows and nobody thinks about them. Good infrastructure is like that: you notice it most when something backs up.

That is usually when people find us — rarely with a diagnosis, mostly with symptoms. A question answered three times gets asked a fourth. A report that should take an hour takes three days because the numbers live in four places and none agree. A new hire spends their first month learning where things are instead of doing the work. Somewhere there is one person who knows how everything connects — which works beautifully until they’re out sick. Or out the door.

Field note / 01

The work gets heavier long before the problem gets a name.

Those are drips: easy to live with, one at a time, until a departure takes years of know-how away in an afternoon; a deadline arrives and nothing can be found while a funder or board waits; or a system that seemed stable fails the one week it matters. By then the water is on the floor, and everything costs more.

Risk / 02

A system can look stable while one person quietly holds it together.

The drips sound familiar? Tell us what’s happening

When you call us, we trace the lines. We follow how information moves through your organization — where it pools, where it leaks, where two systems were never connected and someone is hauling buckets between them by hand. We clear what’s stuck and connect what belongs together, so information is entered once and travels where it needs to go.

Then we hand you the wrench. We show your team how the system works, where the shutoff valves are, and what to listen for when something sounds off. We build capacity, not dependency. The pipes are yours; your team should be able to run them without keeping us on retainer forever.

Handoff / 03

The best infrastructure leaves your team more capable, not more dependent.

You don’t need to know what the problem is called before you reach out. If the repeated questions, the buckets, or the one indispensable person sound familiar, that’s enough. Call while it’s still a small job.
